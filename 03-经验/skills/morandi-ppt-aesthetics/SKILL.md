# Sisu PPT 审美与排版

This is aesthetic direction, not a PowerPoint implementation library. Use the available Presentations skill for native authoring and rendering. For TalentsAI tier selection, also use `talentsai-office-three-tier`. Current user instructions and supplied visual references override these historical preferences.

## Read the relevant evidence first

Read [the casebook](references/casebook.md) before choosing a style for Sisu. It records actual pages, user corrections and the limits of each example. Open the available case images, not only the descriptions. For the project-level review and source evidence, follow the links in the casebook.

For new compositions or a substantial redesign, also read [the user-confirmed art-exhibition calibration](references/art-exhibition-calibration.md). It records the latest approved reference and how to use image generation without returning to a fixed template. The skill's historical name does not prescribe a muted palette, minimalism or a native-shapes-only implementation.

If the historical files are unavailable or have moved, use the written observations as provisional guidance, locate current user references when possible, and do not claim to have inspected images you could not open. Missing historical assets are not a reason to halt an otherwise clear task.

The goal is a complete presentation for its audience, not a collection of well-formatted information blocks. A visually clean file can still be an ordinary draft. Do not call it a polished reference because it is editable, opens correctly or passes a platform beauty check.

## Make the brief tangible

Before layout, identify the actual audience, venue, purpose and essential information. A tourist gathering needs itinerary and purchase conditions; a fragrance salon needs credible services and timing; a ceramics talk needs details large enough to observe. Do not make realistic content thinner just to create sparse, attractive pages.

From the relevant examples, state a few observable goals and how they fit this topic. For example: photos and text share one composition; service pages keep the visual language of product pages; comparison pages reveal the particular difference being explained. Do not use only labels such as premium, elegant, professional or Morandi.

Do not force every theme into travel advertising or a dark luxury style. In a bright room or an evidence-heavy page, a light layout can be better. The Poetry City example shows that pure-color pages can work when hierarchy, palette and rhythm are deliberate.

## Let content determine the composition and the medium

Before constructing boxes, identify what this page helps the audience understand and what visible evidence makes that understanding possible. A detail page should reveal a particular feature, not merely show a large object beside a general impression. Preserve enough explanation to connect observation and meaning; a sequence of slogans is not a complete talk.

Choose a composition that expresses that relationship: viewing distance, whole and detail, before and after, scale, sequence or spatial context. Related pages may share a structure when that helps comparison. Do not reuse a fixed container arrangement merely because it is easy to implement.

The user explicitly welcomes image generation for backgrounds, textures, foregrounds and visual components. Choose the visual result first, then use generation, authentic photography, cutouts, native objects or a hybrid as appropriate. When generation materially improves the composition, use the available image-generation skill/tool rather than approximating every effect with rectangles. A complete raster mockup can explore a direction; an editable final deck must still satisfy its actual editability contract.

Design generated assets for their final placement, with compatible lighting, perspective, palette and usable text space. Generate transparent components when separation helps. Keep documented objects, essential facts and explanatory evidence authentic; generated atmosphere must not redraw a ceramic pattern or become fake event documentation. Do not substitute attractive invented evidence for missing detail. Keep required editable text, data and annotations separate from decorative imagery.

## Design the whole picture

- Choose the visual anchor before fixing text boxes. Look at the subject's position, camera angle, existing light, useful negative space and required evidence. Do not default to a half-page text box plus a disconnected rectangular photograph.
- Prefer an integrated photo composition when it suits the content. Use the photograph's quiet area, a directional gradient or a restrained translucent support to make native text legible. Keep the important subject visible. Avoid wide opaque black bars that flatten scenery.
- Layer for a reason: photo, local tonal treatment, occasional translucent surface, editable information. A layer should improve depth, contrast or grouping. Do not turn all content into shadowed cards.
- Judge the visible object, not the dimensions of its container. Equal picture boxes can contain differently scaled subjects or inconsistent white margins. Preserve complete objects where required; pair them with true detail crops when explaining a part.
- Let palette and image treatment come from the actual material: posters, products, ceramic glazes or place photography. Match tone and visual weight across new imagery. Do not apply an unrelated stock style midway through the deck.
- Preserve original evidence. For artwork, historical objects and documented events, use authentic supplied or licensed images. Generated scenery may support an allowed concept, but must not silently replace real objects or evidence.

## Give the deck rhythm

Change page architecture when the narrative job changes: establishing image, single-object observation, detail study, comparison, route, timeline, table or closing. Use a small coherent visual vocabulary, not a different template on every page.

Product or scenery pages must not be polished while schedules, pricing, service and questions fall back to plain black lists. Those information pages deserve an appropriate image strategy or a deliberately designed light information treatment.

Repeated structure can help compare related products. Check the purpose before changing it. Unrelated topics should not all inherit identical two-column or three-card arrangements. Empty space must establish focus, pause or hierarchy; it is not evidence of quality by itself.

Keep typography disciplined but expressive in scale. Use fonts that are actually available for QA. Let the main title, useful image and necessary information dominate; remove unnecessary labels and corner fragments. Do not fill weak composition with English kickers, rules, pills or giant slide numbers.

## Write useful, natural slide copy

Use the vocabulary of the actual work. Keep audience-facing slides free of production status, evaluation language and instructions to the model. Sources belong where accurate attribution is appropriate, usually notes for supporting references, not a new unrelated page interrupting the talk.

Avoid repeating the same slogan, three-part rhythm or question pattern across every slide. A plain subject title often works. Give the audience concrete facts, distinctions and practical conditions instead of atmospheric phrases. Do not promise undetectability or falsify authorship.

## Review against the user's examples and the actual alternatives

For a substantial redesign, render a few representative key pages early: include both an image-led page and a difficult information or comparison page. Use them to settle composition before rebuilding every slide. This is an early check, not a mandatory new approval gate when the user has authorized independent execution.

Review the composite at presentation size, not assets in isolation. Check that the page communicates its specific point, the image leads somewhere, and foreground/background relations are intentional. Intentional overlap can create depth; accidental collisions between type, borders, subject edges and panels are defects. The accepted reference is a quality comparison, not a layout to reproduce on every page.

Inspect the full deck overview for rhythm and the relevant full-size pages for legibility and detail. For a three-tier task, put matching subjects from the reference and second tier side by side at the same display size. Record where either one is stronger. Retain useful evidence from the weaker-looking draft when improving the reference; do not alter a model output to manufacture a gap.

Keep two results separate:

1. **Basic usability:** source accuracy, content coverage, native editability, font/render behavior, file size and obvious defects.
2. **Visual quality:** focal point, integration, topic-specific imagery, purposeful density, cross-page rhythm and fine composition.

A technical pass does not imply an aesthetic pass. If the reference only removes boxes or uses a quieter palette, and the alternative explains details better, the claimed tier-three advantage is not established. Improve the reference or say the distinction is insufficient; do not write flattering tier notes to rescue the decision.

After material edits, re-render the actual current file. Keep PPTX, PDF, screenshots and explanations aligned. Preserve old source files; use simple deliverable names such as 标准答案.pptx and store version history privately.

## Retrospective

When the user requests a retrospective, save the observed page, correction, transferable principle and counterexample. Separate explicit user preferences from your own design inference. Record exact source paths and hashes so another agent can locate the same version. Update this skill narrowly when new evidence changes decisions; do not accumulate every defect as a universal prohibition.
